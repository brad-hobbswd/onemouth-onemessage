CHAPTER 2

Words That Were Never Meant to Travel

Some words never stay where they begin.

"The tongue can no man tame. It is an unruly evil, full of deadly poison." James 3:8

Maya used to believe that words were harmless once they left her mouth. She believed intention mattered more than outcome. She believed that if her heart was right, the words would land right.

She would learn otherwise.

The morning began like many others. Eli sat at the kitchen table, his backpack slumped against the chair leg, his homework untouched. He stared at the page as though it were written in another language.

"I tried," he said quietly when Maya approached. "I just can't do it fast like the others."

Maya swallowed hard. She kissed the top of his head and spoke reassurance, but her heart carried a deeper fear. She had shared that fear once. Only once. She had needed relief. Someone to hear her.

Rachel's kitchen smelled like coffee and cinnamon the day Maya opened her heart.

"I just need prayer," Maya said. "Eli is struggling, and I don't want him labeled."

Rachel nodded. She listened. She sympathized. She agreed.

What Maya did not see was how quickly compassion turned into conversation. How prayer turned into opinion. How details multiplied as they traveled.

Within a week, Maya felt the change. The glances. The subtle distance. The forced politeness that replaced warmth.

At a school pickup, she overheard a whisper.

"That's him. The one with issues."

Maya froze.

That night, Eli cried in his room.

"They think I'm bad," he said. "They talk about me."

Maya felt something tear inside her. She had not spoken cruelly, but her words had still carried poison once they were no longer covered by love.

She sat alone in the dark after Eli fell asleep. Her Bible lay open on the bed.

James 3:8 stared back at her: "The tongue can no man tame. It is an unruly evil, full of deadly poison."

She whispered, "Jesus, I didn't mean for this."

The room grew still. Not empty. Still.

Then it happened.

She saw herself standing in a wide place that felt both vast and heavy. The ground beneath her feet was scattered with words turned solid. Each one bore familiar phrases. Spoken casually. Repeated carelessly.

Every step hurt.

She knelt and cried out the name of Jesus.

The words began to crack. Light broke through them. What once crushed her began to dissolve into something living.

When she woke, her pillow was wet with tears, but her chest felt lighter.

The next morning, she made a choice that required courage. She confronted Rachel, not with anger, but with truth.

"What I shared needed covering," Maya said softly. "It needed prayer, not repetition."

Rachel wept. She admitted how far the story had gone. How quickly it had grown.

Repentance followed. Apologies came slowly. Healing slower still.

Something had shifted in the spirit.

Teachers became gentler. Parents became more aware. Eli began to speak again.

One afternoon he came home smiling.

"My teacher said I explain things really well," he said.

Maya knew God had redeemed what words had damaged.

She learned a holy fear that day. Words never stay where they are spoken. They either heal or haunt.
