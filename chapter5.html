CHAPTER 5

The Sanctuary Where Words Still Wounded

Some wounds come from voices that once felt safest.

"Therewith bless we God, even the Father; and therewith curse we men... these things ought not so to be."

James 3:9–10

Church had always felt safe to Janelle, until something changed. For years she sat in the third row every Sunday with a notebook open and her Bible resting quietly on her lap. Worship moved her deeply. Prayer mattered to her. The presence of God felt like home. Yet recently something unsettled her spirit. A heaviness lingered that she could not ignore.

The shift began quietly. Small comments. Passing observations. Conversations that felt harmless at first.

"Did you hear about the worship leader?"

"I'm just discerning."

"We should pray about leadership."

The phrases sounded spiritual. The tone carried something else entirely.

Janelle noticed how quickly concern became speculation. Prayer requests suddenly carried details that felt unnecessary. Opinions slowly disguised themselves as wisdom.

She tried ignoring the feeling. After all, this was church. People loved Jesus here. People worshiped here. People prayed here.

One Wednesday evening she overheard a conversation while walking through the hallway.

"I don't think Pastor Samuel hears God like he used to," someone whispered.

"Yeah. Something feels different. Maybe pride."

Janelle froze.

Pastor Samuel baptized her. He prayed with her through grief. He stood beside families during difficult seasons. Perfect? No. Sincere? Absolutely.

The words spoken against him felt heavy. Wrong.

Sleep never came easily that night. Janelle opened her Bible and slowly read James 3:9–10: "Therewith bless we God, even the Father; and therewith curse we men, which are made after the similitude of God... these things ought not so to be."

Quietly she whispered: "Jesus, why does this happen here too?"

Then the vision came.

She saw the sanctuary filled with worshipers. Hands lifted. Voices raised. Music filled the room. Above them hovered words spoken throughout the week. Some glowed softly. Others dripped like acid.

Wherever those words landed, the body beneath weakened.

Janelle gasped awake.

The following Sunday Pastor Samuel stood at the pulpit looking visibly tired. Something in his face carried weariness. After service she approached him quietly.

"I just want you to know something," she said gently. "Your obedience matters. God sees your heart."

Pastor Samuel's eyes filled with tears.

"You have no idea how much I needed that," he said softly.

Later that week Janelle felt a quiet prompting during prayer meeting. Not accusation. Not exposure. Only truth.

"We cannot worship with the same mouths we wound," she said. "When we love Jesus, our words should reflect Him."

The room fell silent. Not dramatic silence. Convicting silence.

Repentance followed. Not loudly. Not emotionally. Real repentance.

Pastor Samuel preached the following Sunday about guarding the tongue. He spoke honestly about his own struggles and the atmosphere within the sanctuary slowly began changing. Safety returned. Healing returned.

Janelle learned something powerful. Church wounds often come through familiar voices. Healing often arrives through them too. Continue reading and discover how grace responds when words are aimed directly against someone who never deserved them.
